About

At the boundary of artificial and biological intelligence.

I am an MIT Master of Engineering student studying Computer Science and Neuroscience. My academic path sits between EECS, Brain and Cognitive Sciences, chemistry, and biology, with a broad interest in how intelligent systems can be understood and engineered.

I think about intelligence in both directions: how AI can help us ask sharper questions about neural and biological systems, and how neuroscience can suggest new principles for robust, adaptive, and useful AI.

My interests include computational neuroscience, statistical and deep learning methods for biological signal processing, AI for personalized medicine and drug discovery, and the scientific study of cognition and consciousness.

Research

Questions I keep returning to.

01

AI for neuroscience and biology

Building and applying computational tools that make complex biological and neural data more interpretable, predictive, and useful for scientific discovery.

02

Learning from neural systems

Studying how ideas from perception, cognition, neural dynamics, and biological adaptation can inform the design of more capable AI systems.

03

Signals, structure, and inference

Exploring statistical and deep learning approaches for extracting structure from noisy biological time series, neural recordings, and other high-dimensional signals.
