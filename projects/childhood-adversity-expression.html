Adverse childhood experiences · RNA sequencing and behavior

Cell-Type-Specific Transcriptomic Effects of Adverse Childhood Experiences in the Human Prefrontal Cortex

We pair an analysis of gene expression in the aging human brain with a study of behavior in mice raised with limited nesting and bedding.

Motivation

People with different childhood histories can show differences in gene expression decades later. We asked which cell types in the aging prefrontal cortex carry those differences. A companion mouse study let us examine an early-life condition directly by measuring how mothers and offspring behaved when nesting material and bedding were limited.

Prior work

The Religious Orders Study and Rush Memory and Aging Project pair participants’ life histories with donated brain tissue. Mathys and colleagues used RNA from individual nuclei in these cohorts to identify cell-type-specific patterns associated with Alzheimer’s disease. We applied that approach to childhood adversity and used Motion Sequencing to characterize recurring movements in the mouse recordings.

What we delivered

I compared three measures of childhood experience across 19 human cell types, separating changes in gene expression from changes in cell proportions. A follow-up focused on parvalbumin-positive basket cells, a class of inhibitory neurons. In the mouse study, I measured how often mothers and offspring used each movement and which movements tended to follow one another.
