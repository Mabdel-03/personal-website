How does early housing shape mouse behavior?

Depth video reveals the short movements that make up a mouse’s behavior and the order in which they occur.

Motivation

Two mice can travel the same distance while spending their time very differently. We wanted to know whether standard housing, enrichment, social isolation, or limited nesting and bedding are associated with differences in the movements mothers and offspring make. We also asked whether their movements tend to follow one another in different sequences.

Prior work

Wiltschko and colleagues introduced Motion Sequencing (MoSeq), which learns recurring fragments of mouse movement from depth video. Each fragment, called a syllable, receives a numerical label. We fit separate models for mothers and offspring. Within the offspring model, a label describes the same learned movement across housing groups, so its frequency can be compared across recordings.

What we found

I produced syllable counts, transition matrices, and behavioral profiles for the housing groups. In the female offspring recordings shown here, syllable 00 accounted for 4.6% of counts in the social-isolation group and 3.6% in standard housing. The table lets you inspect the corresponding numbers in male offspring and see how many movement counts each percentage summarizes.
