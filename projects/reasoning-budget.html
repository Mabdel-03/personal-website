Regime-Dependent Inference-Time Compute Allocation

When should a reasoning model spend its budget on a longer attempt, and when should it sample several answers and vote?

Motivation

A model with more time can work longer on one solution, reconsider a draft, or generate independent answers and vote. Those choices solve different problems. More attempts can uncover a correct answer that none of the others found, yet a majority vote can still discard it.

Prior work

Self-consistency samples several reasoning paths and selects their most common answer. Self-Refine has a model critique and revise its own output. Work on scaling test-time compute showed that the best use of additional computation can vary with problem difficulty. These approaches motivated a direct comparison under a shared reasoning budget.

What we found

We compared longer reasoning runs, repeated sampling, revision, and multi-agent discussion on reasoning benchmarks. For the model we tested, longer individual runs gave the strongest accuracy on harder groups of questions. For questions with reliable shallow-reasoning solutions, voting could reach the same accuracy with less computation. Tracking which answers were generated, preserved, and finally selected helped explain the difference.
