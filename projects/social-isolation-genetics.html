Social isolation · Human genetics

Cellular Etiology of Social Isolation Traits

Which brain cell types express genes associated with loneliness, frequency of social contact, and the ability to confide in someone?

Motivation

Someone who sees others frequently can still feel lonely or lack a person to confide in. We asked whether these three aspects of social connection have different genetic associations, and whether genes associated with each trait are especially active in particular types of brain cells.

Prior work

Day and colleagues used UK Biobank to study genetic associations with loneliness and social activities. Duncan and colleagues developed a way to compare genome-wide association results with gene-expression profiles across 461 clusters of human brain cells. We brought these approaches together to investigate each social trait in the full cohort and in female and male participants separately.

What we delivered

In our preliminary combined-cohort analysis, loneliness showed enrichment in nine brain cell clusters, including cortical and midbrain populations. Ability to confide and frequency of social contact showed enrichment in six and four clusters, respectively. These comparisons identify cell populations for follow-up. Establishing their role in behavior requires other kinds of evidence.
